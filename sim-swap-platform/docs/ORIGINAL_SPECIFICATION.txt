## 2. Core fraud scenarios

Detect suspicious sequences involving:

- SIM replacement followed by login from an unfamiliar device.
- SIM replacement followed by password reset or account recovery.
- SIM replacement followed by new beneficiary creation and fund transfer.
- Repeated replacement requests within a short period.
- Number porting followed by suspicious account activity.
- Unusual replacement activity associated with a retail outlet or agent, where authorized data exists.
- Changes in device, location, authentication behavior, and transaction velocity.
- Suspicious activity across linked accounts or identifiers where data sharing is permitted.

Distinguish legitimate SIM replacement, eSIM migration, device upgrade, travel, number recycling, and account recovery from fraud.

A SIM change is a risk signal, not proof of fraud. Number verification confirms a network relationship; it does not independently prove the rightful customer controls the account.

Support both:

1. Pre-replacement assessment, when an operator can provide replacement-request data.
2. Post-replacement assessment before login, recovery, beneficiary changes, or transactions.

Keep these modes separate because their available signals differ.

## 4. Data ingestion and telecom integration

Implement adapters for:

- SIM replacement requests and completed replacement events.
- Number porting events.
- Authentication, account recovery, and device events.
- Beneficiary changes and financial transactions.
- Investigation outcomes and confirmed fraud labels.
- Authorized operator SIM swap APIs.

Use a stable, explicitly pinned CAMARA/GSMA-compatible contract where supported. Verify the actual provider’s API version, authentication, capabilities, monitoring window, and error semantics.

Do not invent Ethio telecom, Safaricom Ethiopia, banking, or mobile money endpoints. When actual specifications or credentials are missing, provide a clearly labeled mock adapter and an integration checklist.

Represent these states distinctly:

- Confirmed recent SIM change.
- No change within the provider’s monitored window.
- Unknown or unavailable information.
- Unsupported operation.
- Stale information.
- Provider timeout or outage.

Never translate an unavailable or null response into “no SIM change.”

For every event, record:

- Tenant and source.
- Unique event ID and schema version.
- Event time and ingestion time.
- Pseudonymous subscriber/account identifiers.
- Event type.
- Authorized attributes.
- Signal provenance, freshness, and quality.

Implement validation, deduplication, idempotency, replay, dead-letter handling, and bounded handling of late or out-of-order events.

Use UTC internally. Support configurable phone-number normalization, currencies, and display timezones, including Ethiopian numbers and ETB.

## 5. Feature engineering

Create a shared, versioned feature pipeline for training and serving.

Candidate features include:

- Time since last observed SIM change.
- Replacement counts across configurable windows.
- Device novelty and device-change frequency.
- Time between SIM change and account recovery.
- Time between SIM change and beneficiary creation.
- Login and failed-authentication velocity.
- Transaction amount relative to historical behavior.
- New-beneficiary status and transaction velocity.
- Geographic change, with accuracy and travel context.
- Account tenure and historical activity.
- Authorized agent or outlet behavioral aggregates.
- Missing-signal indicators and signal age.

Use only information available at the moment of the decision.

Prevent future information, investigation outcomes, post-decision actions, and labels from entering predictive features. Historical aggregate features must be computed as of the decision timestamp.

Implement point-in-time training joins and automated training/serving parity checks.

Define feature TTLs, refresh behavior, stale-feature handling, and cold-start behavior. Treat sparse history and unavailable data explicitly.

## 6. Machine learning pipeline

Implement and compare:

1. A transparent rules-only baseline.
2. Logistic regression.
3. A gradient-boosted tree classifier.
4. An optional Isolation Forest anomaly detector.

Use anomaly detection as a supplementary signal, not an unvalidated fraud probability. Do not add deep learning unless available data and benchmarks justify it.

Address:

- Severe class imbalance.
- Delayed and incomplete fraud labels.
- Investigation selection bias.
- False-negative labels hidden among unresolved cases.
- Changing fraud patterns.
- Different behavior across operators and tenants.

Track labels such as confirmed fraud, confirmed legitimate, unresolved, and disputed. Record label provenance and maturation time. Do not automatically label every unreviewed event as legitimate.

Use chronological training, validation, and final holdout periods. Separate linked fraud campaigns or duplicated cases to avoid leakage. Document any unavoidable overlap of recurring subscribers and evaluate performance for both returning and previously unseen subscribers.

Apply resampling or class weighting only within training folds.

Evaluate using:

- Precision-recall AUC.
- Precision and recall at selected thresholds.
- False-positive rate.
- Recall at a fixed analyst-review capacity.
- False positives per 10,000 assessments.
- Calibration and Brier score.
- Expected operational cost under documented assumptions.
- Performance by operator, account tenure, legitimate replacement scenario, and data availability.
- Inference latency and resource consumption.

Include confidence intervals where feasible.

Choose thresholds on validation data using business costs and review capacity; preserve the final holdout for unbiased evaluation.

Generate reproducible reports and model cards covering datasets, limitations, feature versions, calibration, thresholds, and intended use.

If only synthetic data exists, mark all metrics as demonstration results. Synthetic accuracy is not evidence of real-world fraud detection performance.

## 7. Decision engine

Implement a versioned policy engine combining validated model outputs, rules, signal quality, and action context.

Return:

- Assessment ID and assessed action.
- Risk score with documented meaning.
- Calibrated fraud probability only when validated calibration exists.
- Risk band.
- Recommended action.
- Stable reason codes and human-readable explanations.
- Model, feature, and policy versions.
- Signal timestamps, quality, and missing-data indicators.
- Whether fallback behavior was used.
- Decision expiration time.

Supported actions:

- ALLOW.
- STEP_UP.
- REVIEW.
- TEMPORARY_HOLD.
- BLOCK_RECOMMENDATION.

Keep recommendations separate from enforcement. External account or transaction changes require an authorized integration, explicit policy, and auditable execution.

For suspected SIM compromise, do not use SMS to the affected number as the sole step-up factor. Support an existing trusted device, passkey, or independently verified recovery procedure.

Make outage behavior configurable by action type and risk. Do not universally fail open or block every customer.

Account for the time between assessment and execution: bind decisions to the assessed action, expire them, and reassess when relevant signals change. A clean assessment must not authorize unrelated future transactions.

Provide customer recovery, legitimate replacement verification, temporary-hold expiry, and audited manual override workflows.

## 10. Security and privacy

Create a threat model covering:

- Unauthorized subscriber lookup.
- Cross-tenant access.
- Forged or replayed events.
- Insider misuse.
- Label and training-data poisoning.
- Unauthorized model replacement.
- Leakage through logs, dashboards, or exports.
- Webhook abuse and exposed secrets.

Implement:

- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Managed secrets and credential rotation.
- Least-privilege access.
- MFA for privileged users.
- Tenant isolation across databases, caches, events, exports, and storage.
- Keyed pseudonymization for phone numbers and account identifiers; do not rely on unsalted hashes.
- Masking and redaction.
- Append-only, tamper-evident audit records.
- Retention and deletion workflows, including training-data lineage.
- Approval controls for high-impact policy changes.
- Restricted access to model artifacts and explanation endpoints.

Do not collect OTP contents, SMS contents, authentication secrets, or unnecessary subscriber data.

Document legal-basis, consent, retention, and data-sharing requirements for review in the deployment jurisdiction. Do not claim regulatory compliance merely because controls exist.
